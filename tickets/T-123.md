# T-123 — Five chunks were being silently dropped, including every tripmine

**Cycle 11, refinement 3 of 4.** This started as "2 of 132 patterns are
silently dropped by `parse_catalog_lenient`" — a known, parked, low-priority
item. It turned out to be much worse, and it explains why tripmines never
appeared in a generated level.

## The mechanism

`parse_catalog_lenient` (`levelgen.rs:632`) exists so one broken chunk cannot
take the whole catalog down: it parses strictly, and on failure removes the
chunk named in the error and retries. That is a reasonable shipping decision.
The problem is that nothing ever checked *how many* chunks it removed.

Measured with a throwaway example that ran the same loop and printed each drop:

```
DROP: chunk 'ch05_ramp_of_embers' socket 's' has no platform at its walking height ...
DROP: chunk 'pa_ember_fork' has unknown platform kind 'TRIPMINE'
DROP: chunk 'pa_frost_fork' socket 'e' has no platform at its walking height ...
DROP: chunk 'pa4_frost_fork' socket 'b' has no platform at its walking height ...
DROP: chunk 'pa4_ruin_switchback' socket 'b' has no platform at its walking height ...
strict parse OK, 127 chunks
```

**127 of 132 chunks loaded.** Five were gone, silently, in every build.

## The one that matters: tripmines never existed

`platform_kind_from_str` (`levelgen.rs:334`) maps the catalog's kind names to
protocol constants. It knew `CRUMBLE`, `JUMP_PAD`, `TRAMPOLINE`, the four
`RAMP_*` and `NORMAL`. It did **not** know `TRIPMINE`, even though
`protocol::PLATFORM_TRIPMINE` has existed since the hazard wave and
`sim::tripmine` arms it.

So every pattern that used a tripmine was rejected by the strict parser and
dropped by the lenient one. The owner asked for tripmines; they were in the
protocol, in the sim, in the hazard tags, and in the catalog — and never in a
generated level. The `8.hazard_entities` audit passed the whole time because it
reads the JSON dump, not the loaded catalog.

## Fixes

1. **`platform_kind_from_str` learns `TRIPMINE`.** One arm.
2. **`verify_socket` accepts a ramp edge.** It required a platform whose *top
   face* was at the socket's height, which a ramp never has — its walking
   height varies along the incline, so a socket at its low edge sits at
   `min.y`. `ch05_ramp_of_embers` is a single ramp and was dropped for this.
3. **`ch05_ramp_of_embers` gets a landing deck.** With the socket check fixed
   the chunk loaded, and then `5.ramp-anchors` failed: 10 of 124,557 ramps had
   a high edge meeting nothing, because the chunk's top was only anchored if
   the neighbour happened to sit at exactly y=6. A 14x2 u deck at the top
   anchors it unconditionally.
4. **`pa_frost_fork` arms widened 2.5 u -> 3 u.** Its east and west sockets
   were 6 u wide on 2.5 u arms, and the minimum legal socket width is 3.0 u, so
   no legal socket existed. The arms are now 3 u and the north/south decks meet
   them at ±1.5 instead of overlapping.
5. **`pa4_frost_fork` / `pa4_ruin_switchback` socket widths 6 -> 4.** Their end
   arms are 4 u deep.
6. **`pa_ember_fork` east/west sockets moved to z=-2.** They straddled the
   NORMAL/TRIPMINE seam, so no single platform touched the edge across their
   width.
7. **`pb_mine_plaza` east/west sockets moved to z=-3.** Same seam problem, in
   the pattern added by T-122.

## The alarm that was missing

```rust
#[test]
fn every_shipped_chunk_parses_strictly() {
    let strict = parse_catalog(&merged).expect("every shipped chunk must parse strictly");
    let lenient = parse_catalog_lenient(&merged).expect("lenient parse must also succeed");
    assert_eq!(strict.chunks.len(), lenient.chunks.len(), ...);
    assert_eq!(strict.chunks.len(), 135, "catalog chunk count changed");
}
```

The strict parse must succeed, the lenient parse must not remove anything, and
the count is pinned so a chunk that stops being included at all is caught too.
This is the check that would have caught the tripmine loss on the day it
happened.

## Tuning notes

- `5.reachability` now reports 252 unreachable platforms (scenery, informational)
  and a tightest rejected jump of 7.00 u at a −9.50 u rise. Both are inside the
  envelope; nothing to do.
- The catalog is 135 chunks and the count is now pinned by a test. Adding a
  pattern means bumping that number deliberately, which is the point.
- `ch05_ramp_of_embers` is a 6 u rise over a 15 u run — the steepest ramp in the
  catalog at 0.4 rise/run against a 0.5 limit. Worth watching in play.
