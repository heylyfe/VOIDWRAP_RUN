# T-158 — Vertical routing: the bot learns to climb

**Cycle:** 25
**Status:** PASS — a level is emptied by walking; `level-completion` is a live PASS
**Area:** `tools/playthrough/run.mjs` (tool-only; no server changes)
**Model:** deepseek-v4-max, thinking max

## Why this ticket exists

T-156 fixed two real measurement bugs (the bot fed the server 80 input frames/s into a
60 frames/s drain, and air control aimed at the point directly below the bot). Gift rate went
30.7% -> 58.0% and deaths went to zero. What it did **not** do is empty a level, and the
`level-completion` check has SKIPped for two cycles with the reason recorded in the gate output:

> the remaining blocker is vertical routing -- a gift directly above or below the bot has no
> route, so the bot blacklists it and holds.

That is the last coverage hole between this suite and a real end-to-end. A level-completion E2E
is the backbone of every future audit, so it is worth real effort.

## The measured root cause (read this before you design anything)

I traced it. It is not "the bot is greedy". It is that **the platform graph cannot represent a
climb**, and the game ships two platform kinds whose entire purpose is to climb.

`tools/playthrough/run.mjs`:

- `maxGapForRise(rise)` returns **0 for any rise above +2.0 u**. `buildPlatformGraph` therefore
  creates no edge to any platform more than 2.0 u above the bot. The comment above it says the
  envelope "mirrors `sim::levelgen::design_max_gap`", which is true for a *jump* and false for the
  level as a whole.
- `findLanding` rejects any landing with `top - y > JUMP_APEX - 0.2` (2.05 u), so a launch-platform
  landing is never even considered.
- `nearestLanding` (air control) picks the nearest platform at or below the bot. During a launch
  that is the platform it just left, so air control actively steers the bot back down.

The two launch kinds, from `server/crates/protocol/src/generated.rs` and
`server/crates/sim/src/tuning.rs`:

| kind | constant | launch velocity | apex (`v^2 / 2g`, g = 32.0) |
|---|---|---|---|
| `PLATFORM_JUMP_PAD` = 2 | `PAD_LAUNCH_VEL` | 16.0 | **+4.00 u** |
| `PLATFORM_TRAMPOLINE` = 7 | `TRAMPOLINE_LAUNCH_VEL` | 26.0 | **+10.56 u** |

`PAD_HORIZONTAL_VEL_MUL = 0.6`, `TRAMPOLINE_HORIZONTAL_VEL_MUL = 1.15`, `RUN_SPEED = 9.0`.
A jump reaches +2.25 u. So the graph's 2.0 u ceiling makes **every platform reachable only by a
pad or a trampoline invisible to the router** -- and those are exactly the platforms a gift sits
on when the bot gives up.

## What to build

1. **Model launch platforms as graph connectors.** An edge A -> B exists when A contains (or is) a
   launch platform whose apex covers B's rise and whose horizontal reach covers the gap. Mirror the
   two launch velocities in the bot with a comment naming `tuning.rs`, the way `GRAVITY` and
   `JUMP_VELOCITY` already are. Keep the existing jump edges; this is additive.
2. **Route through them.** When the next hop is a launch platform, aim at its centre and do **not**
   press jump -- contact launches. When airborne off a launch, hold the launch direction until the
   apex, then aim at the target. Air control must stop fighting the launch.
3. **Blacklist only what the graph says is unreachable.** A gift the graph can reach from the bot's
   current platform must never be blacklisted. The blacklist stays as the last resort for genuinely
   unreachable gifts, and it must not be the thing that ends the run.
4. **`level-completion` becomes a live PASS** when a level is emptied by walking. Keep the SKIP
   shape and the number in the detail if it still cannot. Do not turn it into a FAIL.

### What changed, and why

The ticket's diagnosis was right about the symptom and incomplete about the cause. Launch
platforms were one of **four** independent reasons the bot could not empty a level. Each was
measured, fixed, and re-measured; each fix exposed the next.

1. **The graph and the landing probe used different envelopes.** `buildPlatformGraph` used
   `maxGapForRise` (levelgen's design envelope, 6.0 u flat) while `findLanding` used `jumpReach`
   (5.06 u). Seven pairs on seed-7 level 1 were graph-reachable but probe-unreachable, including
   the 6.0 u flat gap P9<->P19 on the route to half the level. The bot routed at a gap it could not
   clear, `findLanding` returned null, and it blacklisted the gift. Both now use one envelope.

2. **`findLanding` measured the gap from the edge, not from the bot.** It checked
   `d - from <= jumpReach(rise)`, on the theory that the bot jumps *at* the edge. It does not: it
   presses jump as soon as the edge is within `PLAYER_HALF_WIDTH + EDGE_JUMP_DISTANCE` (0.9 u), so
   it leaves the ground up to 0.9 u early and must cover the whole `d`. The check was optimistic by
   exactly that run-up. Now `d <= jumpReach(rise)`, and the router uses a matching
   `jumpGap(rise) = jumpReach(rise) - 0.9` so the two cannot disagree again.

3. **`jumpReach` clamped negative rises, so drops were modelled as flat.** `design_max_gap` admits
   7.0 u at a -4.0 u drop; a clamped reach caps at 5.06 u whatever the drop. `rise` is now signed
   in both `jumpReach` and `launchReach` -- falling further takes longer, so it extends the reach.

4. **`JUMP_SAFETY` had to rise from 0.75 to 0.90.** With the honest `d` check, the widest gap the
   bot can clear is `jumpReach(rise) - 0.9`. At 0.75 that is 4.16 u on the flat, *narrower* than
   the 5.0 u flat gap `levelgen::can_jump_or_step` admits (`design_max_gap(0) = 6.0` minus its
   1.0 u comfort margin) -- so the bot would refuse edges the generator guarantees are jumpable.
   At 0.90 the envelope is a strict superset of the generator's admission at every rise band:

   | rise | -4.0 | -3.0 | -2.0 | -1.0 | 0.0 | +0.5 | +1.0 | +1.5 | +2.0 |
   |---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
   | levelgen admits | 7.00 | 6.50 | 6.00 | 5.50 | 5.00 | 4.50 | 4.00 | 3.50 | 3.00 |
   | bot `jumpGap` | 7.20 | 6.78 | 6.31 | 5.79 | 5.17 | 4.82 | 4.40 | 3.89 | 3.15 |

   The remaining 10% is the real margin: the bot now feeds clock-paced 60 Hz input and holds the
   jump through the rise, so its execution is far closer to the theoretical arc than when 0.75 was
   chosen.

5. **The platform graph was undirected, so the router sent the bot UP drops.** `gap <= jumpGap(up)
   || gap <= jumpGap(down)` created a bidirectional edge when *either* direction worked. On seed-7
   level 2 that killed the bot: P36 (top -10.25) and P28 (top 0.00) are 10.25 u apart, the only
   edge between them is the 4.12 u drop off P28, and the undirected graph routed the bot from P36
   up to P28. It jumped at a platform 10 u above it, hit P34's side wall, slid along it and fell to
   `kill_y`. Edges are now directed: `gap <= jumpGap(up)` is i -> j, `gap <= jumpGap(down)` is
   j -> i. Both levels keep every platform and gift reachable (44/44 and 92/92).

6. **The graph models gaps in 2D and knows nothing about what stands between them.** After fix 5
   the bot died again on level 2, this time jumping at the landing shelf P35 (top -8.50) with P34
   (top -7.50, footprint covering all of P35) in the way. The bot's apex is -8.00, below P34's top,
   so it hit P34's side and fell. New `arcBlocked` samples the jump parabola's own height at each
   probe step and rejects a landing the arc cannot reach without passing through a platform.

### Choices made without a human

- **`JUMP_SAFETY` 0.75 -> 0.90.** Forced by fix 2: below 0.874 the bot's envelope is narrower than
  the generator's admission and it blacklists gifts the level guarantees are reachable. 0.90 is
  the smallest round value that clears every band with margin.
- **`jumpGap` as a separate helper rather than inlining the subtraction.** The router and the probe
  must use the same number; a named function is the only way to keep them from drifting apart
  again, which is what caused fix 1.
- **Directed edges rather than a per-direction cost.** A directed graph is the smallest model that
  can express "you can drop down but not climb up". BFS in `nextHop` already follows `adj[cur]`, so
  nothing else changed.
- **`arcBlocked` skips ramps and the platform the bot is standing on.** The first version skipped
  neither and turned level 1 into 40/46 with 19812 `no-landing` frames: a ramp's AABB is the whole
  slope, so it "contains" the bot standing on it and blocked every jump taken from one. Ramps are
  walkable, so they never block; the platform being left is not an obstacle to leaving it.
- **`arcBlocked` uses `RUN_SPEED` for the arc's horizontal speed.** The controller sets horizontal
  velocity straight from the input every frame, so that is the honest number. The trace's measured
  air speed reads lower (~7.8 u/s) because the arc aim rotates and the snapshot positions are
  quantised, which makes the chord sum an underestimate; the envelope is not tuned to it.
- **The `death-log` check stays informational.** It already reports `true` for any death count by
  design ("a death is not a failure"), so the 8-client enemy deaths do not redden the gate. Left as
  is rather than reclassified, because the ticket's zero-death requirement is attached to the
  1-client 240 s measurement, which is clean.

### Parked

- **Enemy deaths on level 2 in the 8-client sweep (5 of 8 clients).** The bot has no combat logic:
  it walks at gifts and never shoots, dodges or retreats. Before T-158 it never left level 1, so
  this was invisible. Fixing it is a combat-behaviour ticket, not a vertical-routing one, and the
  ticket is tool-only. Recorded here rather than attempted.
- **`phase-coverage` now reports 2 phases `[0,1]` instead of 3 `[0,1,3]`.** Not a regression: the
  run used to reach `PHASE_OVER` because the bot died and ended the run. It now survives, so the
  run is still playing when the 240 s window closes. The check's threshold is `>= 2` and it passes.
