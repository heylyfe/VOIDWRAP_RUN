# T-139 — Why the playthrough bots never reach level 2

**Cycle:** 16 · **Agent:** orchestrator · **Status:** PASS (diagnosis) / PARKED (the check)

`level-progression` has been skipped for many cycles with the note "bots fall before
level 2". T-136 established that the falls are navigation, not collision. This ticket
finds out *why* the navigation fails, and whether the check can be unblocked.

## The bot is not naive

`tools/playthrough/run.mjs` already has a real navigation bot: nearest-gift steering
with hysteresis, a platform-graph route (`nextHop`), an edge probe that jumps at a
landing within 5.5 u or blacklists the gift and holds still, air control toward the
nearest landing, and stuck detection. It is not a random walk. So the failure is
subtle, and guessing at it would have been wrong.

## Measurement

Same server, same seed family, varying only the client count and the duration:

| clients | seconds | deaths | best client's gifts | level reached |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 120 | **0** | 12/46 | 1 |
| 1 | 280 | 1 | 7/46 | 1 |
| 2 | 90 | 2 | 5/52 | 1 |
| 4 | 90 | 3 | 14/64 | 1 |
| 8 | 90 | 7 | 40/88 | 1 |
| 8 | 240 | 8 | 38/88 | 1 |

Three things fall out:

1. **Deaths scale with client count.** One client survives 120 s untouched; eight
   clients lose seven players in 90 s.
2. **Gift count scales with client count** (46 -> 52 -> 64 -> 88), and the bot's
   herd-spreading deliberately sends bot *i* to the *i*-th nearest gift. With eight
   bots, bot 7 targets the 8th-nearest gift, which is across the level. More clients
   means longer routes, which means more chances to fall.
3. **More time does not help.** 240 s produced 38/88, the same plateau as 90 s at
   40/88. The bots are not slow, they are stuck.

## Why it cannot pass

Two independent blockers, both real:

**A. The first fall ends the run.** `Run::new` starts `lives: 0` and the only spare is
the rare `spare_wick` upgrade, so every death in the log has `out: true`. The check
requires the team to collect *all* gifts, which requires all eight bots to survive.
With eight bots wandering a level with edges, that is not achievable. This is the
T-130 tuning note, and it is a **balance decision the owner deferred**, not a defect.

**B. The bot stalls.** Solo, it collected 7 of 46 gifts in 280 s. The edge probe's
failure path is `blacklist the gift, return hold` — the bot stands still. When every
remaining gift is behind a gap the probe cannot find a landing for, the bot holds
still until the blacklist expires, tries again, and holds still again. That is why
more time does not help.

## Disposition

**Parked**, with the reason now precise instead of "bots fall":

- The check is blocked by the starting-lives balance decision (blocker A), which is
  the owner's to make. It becomes testable the moment a run starts with a spare life.
- Blocker B is a harness-quality problem, not a game problem. Fixing it means giving
  the bot a real pathfinder over the platform graph instead of a straight-line steer
  with an edge probe — a substantial piece of work with no effect on the shipped game.

Neither is worth grinding. The check stays skipped, and the skip message now points at
the balance decision rather than implying a bug.

## What this rules out

- **Not a collision bug.** T-136: zero seam falls, every `ground_inset` negative.
- **Not player-vs-player pushing.** The sim has no player-player collision; the
  controller collides only against `World`.
- **Not a time limit.** 240 s plateaus at the same gift count as 90 s.
- **Not gift scarcity.** Gift count scales with players and the bots collect a
  reasonable fraction of what they can reach.

## Tuning notes (not changed)

- Starting `lives: 0` makes the first fall of a run fatal. This is the single largest
  obstacle to automated end-to-end coverage of voting, the shop and altars, because
  all three sit behind level 2. Starting at 1 would unblock them.
- The bot's `hold` fallback is the wrong default. Holding still is safe but makes no
  progress; a bot that walks back toward the level centre would at least keep
  collecting. Worth doing if the harness is ever revisited.
