## Why the old pair collided

Both models were tall rounded columns. Peal was a 1.24-wide bell 1.15 tall; Coil was a 1.24-wide
spring 1.46 tall. Under a bbox-normalised comparison they were nearly the same outline, and the
0.780 was honest — a player at gameplay distance saw two vertical blobs.

The redesign pushes them to opposite corners of the shape space:

- **Peal** — a squat flared trapezoid, 1.79 wide and 1.45 tall, with the clapper hanging clear
  below the lip. The outline is wide-at-the-bottom, narrow-at-the-top, with a bottom-centre tail.
- **Coil** — a 1.20-wide flat disc under a 0.56-wide column 1.70 tall. The outline is a "T".

Nothing about either enemy's behaviour, hitbox, accent or telegraph changed.

## Blender pipeline (new, per the owner's amendment)

The "no binary assets" rule is amended for models: geometry may be authored headlessly by Blender
scripts that live in the repo, with the exported `.glb` committed alongside as a build artifact.
**The script is the source of truth.** CI never runs Blender — the gates validate the result.

- `tools/blender/run.mjs` — wrapper. Finds `blender.exe` via `BLENDER_EXE` or the default install,
  runs `-b --factory-startup --python-exit-code 1 -P <script> -- <repo>`. The
  `--python-exit-code 1` is load-bearing: without it a script that raises still exits 0, which
  turns a broken model into a silent success. Exit codes 0 ok / 1 script failed / 2 setup problem.
- `tools/blender/lib/vw.py` — shared helpers: `start`, `material`, `srgb`, `mesh_object`, `empty`,
  `lathe`, `revolve_solid`, `torus`, `sphere`, `helix`, `set_origin`, `center_origin`,
  `shade_smooth`, `export_glb`, `render_views`, `finish`.
- `tools/blender/scripts/peal.py`, `tools/blender/scripts/coil.py` — the two models.
- `web/public/models/peal.glb` (1,808 triangles), `web/public/models/coil.glb` (2,152 triangles).

## Client side

`Peal.tsx` and `Coil.tsx` keep their animation logic verbatim in intent and now drive named nodes
(`peal_shell_l`, `peal_shell_r`, `peal_clapper`, `peal_lip`, `peal_glow`; `coil_ring_0..4`,
`coil_core`, `coil_base`, `coil_cap`). Each is wrapped in its own `<Suspense fallback={null}>`:
the glb is fetched, and without a boundary one unloaded model would blank the whole canvas.

The first cut cloned the glTF scene per instance and handed it to `<primitive object>`. That is
the obvious approach and it is wrong twice over: `MODEL_COMPONENTS` mounts 24 slots of every
model and one `Object3D` cannot have two parents, and a shared material cannot animate
`emissiveIntensity` per enemy. The clone-and-detach-materials fix worked, but the React Compiler
lint (`react-hooks/immutability`) rejects mutating a `useMemo` value inside `useFrame`, and it is
right to — the other 18 models all keep their mutable three.js objects in refs.

So the shipped version is declarative, matching the rest of the roster: each glTF node's
*geometry* is reused (shared across all 24 slots, which is cheaper than cloning) and its
*translation* is read off the node at render time, while the material is a per-instance JSX
`<meshStandardMaterial>` and the animation drives refs. The Blender script stays the single
source of truth for both shape and placement.

The accent is no longer a literal in the `.tsx`. `enemyLookForBehavior(id)` was added to
`enemyLook.ts` so the models read `ENEMY_LOOKS`, which keeps the palette table the single source
of truth for emissive trim — the thing T-145's uniqueness test guards.

## Choices made without a human

- **Squat bell vs tall spring** rather than two new shapes invented from scratch. Both enemies
  keep their identity (a bell that rings, a spring that winds); only the proportions moved.
- **`center_origin()`** so the bbox centre sits at the model origin. The client places an enemy at
  its hit-sphere centre, so a model authored with its feet on z=0 would sink half a body into the
  floor. This shifts Peal down by 0.12 and Coil down by 0.81 relative to the old hand-built
  models — both are within the redesign's own change and neither moves the hitbox.
- **The silhouette report now writes all 190 pairs**, not the top 40. Looking up one pair by name
  should not require re-running a browser capture.
- **No wholesale refactor of the other 18 models.** The owner's rule is incremental wins only, and
  every other pair is already under the p90.
