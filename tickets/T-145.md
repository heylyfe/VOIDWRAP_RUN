# T-145 — Enemy accent palette: perceptual distinctness, and a guard against table drift

**Cycle:** 20 · **Agent:** orchestrator (deepseek-v4) · **Status:** PASS

## Why

The owner's directive #4 is "make the models look good". Most of that is subjective, but
one part is not: **at gameplay distance the only thing separating one enemy from another
is its accent colour.** The silhouette is a few pixels tall and the fog is already eating
the edges. Two accents that are close in colour read as the same enemy; an accent close to
the fog colour reads as no enemy at all.

Both are measurable. This ticket measures them, fixes what fails, and adds a test so the
palette cannot silently regress.

## The measurement

`web/src/scene/enemyPalette.ts` (new, no three.js/React imports, unit-testable):

- `hexToLab(hex)` — sRGB → CIELAB on the D65 white point.
- `deltaE(a, b)` — CIE76, Euclidean distance in CIELAB.
- `ACCENT_MIN_DELTA_E = 20` — floor between any two enemy accents.
- `FOG_MIN_DELTA_E = 30` — floor between an accent and any theme's fog colour.

RGB distance is the wrong metric and the reason this was never caught: `#ff3355` and
`#e63946` differ by 0x0D in red, which looks like a real difference, but they are only
**10.5** apart in CIELAB — well inside the range where two colours are indistinguishable
side by side.

## What failed, measured over all 20 accents × 4 theme fogs

| pair | delta-E | verdict |
|---|---|---|
| `pallbearer` `#ff3355` vs `peal` `#e63946` | **10.5** | indistinguishable |
| `gale_wight` `#d94fff` vs `mimic` `#ff3df0` | **17.4** | two magentas |
| `snapshrike` `#ff4436` vs `peal` `#e63946` | **17.6** | two reds |
| `pallbearer` `#ff3355` vs `snapshrike` `#ff4436` | **19.7** | two reds |
| `static` `#9aa3ad` vs **ashfall fog** `#6d6873` | **22.5** | grey enemy in grey fog |

`peal` was the worst offender — involved in three of the four closest pairs.

## The fix — four accents, identity preserved

| enemy | before | after | why this colour |
|---|---|---|---|
| `peal` | `#e63946` | `#9d0208` | oxblood. The bell's mouth reads as dark bronze-red, and it is now clearly darker than every other red in the roster. |
| `gale_wight` | `#d94fff` | `#7b2ff7` | deep violet. Still a wind-wraith violet, no longer confusable with `mimic`'s hot magenta. |
| `static` | `#9aa3ad` | `#a8b8c8` | cold steel grey. Pushed cool and lighter so it separates from ashfall's *warm* grey fog while staying grey — its identity. |
| `pallbearer` | `#ff3355` | `#e91e63` | deeper rose. Separates from `peal` and `snapshrike` without leaving the crimson family. |

Result: **closest pair 10.5 → 22.8**, **worst fog contrast 22.5 → 30.3**. Both floors now
hold with headroom.

Every place the colour appears was updated: `ENEMY_LOOKS`, `FALLBACK_LOOK`, the four model
files' `emissive` literals and doc comments, and `design/M3_DESIGN.md`.

## The second finding: the table was not the source of truth

The accents are **duplicated** — `ENEMY_LOOKS` holds the table, and each model hardcodes
its accent as an `emissive` literal. Nothing forced the two to agree, so a palette change
could land in the table and never reach the screen. That is the project's recurring
"the piece exists but never reaches the player" defect class, in colour form.

Two guards in `enemyPalette.test.ts`, both reading the model sources:

1. **`lights each enemy with its own accent or glow`** — every model must render at least
   one of its own two palette colours.
2. **`never renders another enemy's accent`** — no model may render a colour that belongs
   to a different enemy.

Writing guard 1 surfaced a real pre-existing fact worth recording: **`RiftWarden` and
`BoltSentry` light themselves with their *glow*, not their accent.** That is a legitimate
choice (the glow is the hot core), so the guard accepts either. The first version of the
guard used a bare `src.includes(accent)`, which passed on a stale model because the doc
comment above each model also names its accent — it now matches the rendered attribute
specifically.

## Negative tests

| break | expected | actual |
|---|---|---|
| `Peal.tsx` emissive reverted to the old `#e63946` | drift reported | `peal (Peal.tsx) does not render its accent #9d0208` (first version) |
| `Peal.tsx` renders neither accent nor glow | unwired reported | `peal (Peal.tsx) renders neither accent #9d0208 nor glow #ffb0a8` |
| `Peal.tsx` renders `pallbearer`'s accent | theft reported | `peal (Peal.tsx) renders #e91e63, which is pallbearer's accent` |
| palette left unchanged | both floors fail | `static #9aa3ad vs ashfall fog #6d6873: 22.5` + the four close pairs |

## Choice log

- **Fixed the palette rather than the threshold.** The floors (20 / 30) were chosen from
  what the roster can achieve without redesigning identities, then the four offenders were
  moved. Lowering the floor to 10 would have made the metric describe the bug.
- **Did not touch the glows.** They are hot cores seen at close range, not identity
  colours; the accent is what carries identity at distance.
- **Did not restructure `EnemyModelProps` to pass the look down.** That would be the
  architectural fix (20 files, prop plumbing through `EnemyField`), and it is a redesign
  for a problem the test already catches. Logged as a candidate for a human decision.
- **`turret` renders `#ffffff`**, which is `overlook`'s glow. Left alone: pure white is a
  generic highlight, not an identity colour, and guard 2 only covers accents.

## Tuning notes

None — colour only, no gameplay surface. The four new accents are all *darker or cooler*
than what they replaced, so if anything they read slightly less brightly through fog; the
measured fog contrast went up, not down, so this is a net gain.
