# T-143 — `5.reference-scale`: replace the `minExtent` guess with the measured largest platform

**Cycle:** 19 · **Agent:** orchestrator (deepseek-v4) · **Status:** PASS

## Why

`5.reference-scale` reports every pattern whose max XZ footprint falls outside
`[SCALE_EXTENT_MIN, SCALE_EXTENT_MAX]` = `[8, 20]` u. To help a reader tell "out of band
because it is a tall set-piece" from "out of band because someone typed the wrong
number", the message printed a `minExtent` field:

```js
minExtent: f2(2 * (drop / TH.RAMP_MAX_RISE_RUN))
```

That is a guess at the extent the ramp slope cap *forces*: a chunk that drops `d` needs
`2 * d / RAMP_MAX_RISE_RUN` of ramp plus its decks. It was wrong in both directions, and
the message asserted things about the data that were not true.

Deferred three cycles (17, 18, 19 candidates) because it is message honesty, not a
threshold. It is the last known-wrong number in the audit, so it closes here.

## What the old field claimed, and what is actually true

Measured over 400 seeds / 6,000 level records (`/tmp/dump138.ndjson`), the three
out-of-band owners:

| pattern | placements | footprint | drop | old `minExtent` claim | reality |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| `ch06_descent_of_sighs` | 1781 | 30.00 u | 5.50 u | ramp-forced >= 22 u | **genuinely composed**: 2 u deck + 10 u ramp + 6 u deck + 10 u ramp + 2 u deck = 30 u |
| `ch15_tri_crown` | 1415 | 25.00 u | 6.50 u | ramp-forced >= 26 u | **no ramp covers its drop at all** — it is a stepped terrace (0 → −2.5 → −3.5) |
| `ch04_twin_terrace` | 1240 | 22.00 u | 5.00 u | ramp-forced >= 20 u | its ramps sit **inside** its 22 u deck; nothing forces the deck to be 22 u |

So the field was wrong for two of the three owners, and for `ch15_tri_crown` it claimed a
forced extent (26 u) *larger* than the actual footprint (25 u) — i.e. the message said the
chunk was smaller than the minimum it was supposedly forced to be.

## Change

`tools/pattern-audit/run.mjs`:

1. `measurePatternGeometry` tracks `maxPlatform` — the largest single platform's XZ
   extent — alongside the existing whole-footprint `extent`:
   ```js
   maxPlatform = Math.max(maxPlatform, p.max[0] - p.min[0], p.max[2] - p.min[2]);
   ```
2. The `scaleEx` record carries `maxPlatform` instead of `minExtent`.
3. The `5.reference-scale` message reads:
   ```
   ch06_descent_of_sighs:1781 (30.00u footprint, largest platform 8.00u, drop 5.50u)
   ```

`maxPlatform` is the number the reference scale is actually about. It separates the two
cases the old field conflated:

- **big because it is composed of many pieces** — `ch06` (30 u footprint, 8 u largest
  platform) and `ch15` (25 u footprint, 18 u largest platform) are valleys and terraces.
  A player crosses them as a sequence of small steps; nothing about them is oversized.
- **big because one deck is oversized** — `ch04` (22 u footprint, 22 u largest platform)
  is a single 22 u deck, 10% over the 20 u band.

## Choice log

- **Did not reshape `ch04_twin_terrace`.** It is the only genuinely oversized single deck
  (22 u vs a 20 u band, 10% over). Chunks are frozen contracts and the audit is green at
  2.11% against a 10% ceiling; reshaping a frozen chunk for a 10% band overshoot with no
  measured gameplay benefit is exactly the "polish, never redesign" line. Logged as a
  candidate for a human decision instead.
- **Did not delete the field.** A reader still needs to know *why* a pattern is out of
  band. `maxPlatform` answers that with a measurement instead of a formula.
- **Kept `drop`.** It is measured, not guessed, and it is what makes `ch06` legible as a
  descent.
