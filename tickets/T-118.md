# T-118 — Gap scanner: find the jumps that are too big

**Owner decision (2026-11-15):** "lets do it later, basically try and make
something that scans for a gap that is too big to jump."

That replaces the parked playthrough-bot port (F-115-8). The bot was going to
prove reachability *dynamically* by walking a level; this proves it
*statically* by walking the platform graph, which is cheaper, deterministic,
and runs over 6,000 levels instead of one.

## What already existed

`sim::levelgen` already had the whole reachability machine, and it is good:

- `design_max_gap(rise) -> Option<f32>` — the design envelope, edge-to-edge:
  6.0 u on the flat, 4.0 u at a +2.0 u rise, 8.0 u at a −4.0 u drop, `None`
  above +2.0 u (the jump apex is exactly +2.25 u, so a +2.25 u rise can never
  be cleared with room to spare).
- `edge_rise(from, to)` — the rise for a directed edge, sampling where two
  footprints actually meet so a ramp is walkable from the deck it rests on.
- `can_jump_or_step(from, to)` — the edge predicate: touching walk/step/drop,
  or a design-envelope jump minus a 1.0 u comfort margin, with a ≥ 2.0 u
  landing extent, plus jump-pad launches to +3.5 u within 4.5 u.
- `platform_graph(level)` — the directed graph, already public.
- `validate(level)` — refuses a level whose gifts, altars or beacon are
  unreachable from the spawn.

So the scanner did not need new physics. It needed the graph's verdict
**exposed and measured**, because `validate` only checks *gifts*, not
*platforms* — which is exactly why `pb_machine_gauntlet`'s three 4.5/5.5 u
pillars survived: they carry no gift, so nothing ever looked at them.

## What was added

`sim::levelgen::reachability_report(&LevelData) -> ReachabilityReport`:

```rust
pub struct ReachabilityReport {
    pub unreachable: Vec<usize>,          // platform indices the spawn cannot reach
    pub unreachable_gifts: usize,         // gifts sitting on one of those
    pub near_miss: Option<(f32, f32, f32)>, // (gap, rise, excess) of the closest rejected jump
}
pub const NEAR_MISS_RANGE: f32 = 12.0;    // beyond this a pair was never a candidate
```

`near_miss` is the number the owner asked for: among rejected pairs close
enough to have been candidates, the one whose gap sits **closest to its own
design limit**. `excess` is how far past the envelope it sits, so a small
excess is a gap that looks jumpable and is not — the interesting case.

The reported direction is the one closest to legal, which is not always the
"forward" one: a drop allows a wider gap than a rise, so the return trip can
be the tighter rejection. The rise is signed from the take-off platform.

## Wiring

- `pattern_dump.rs` emits `unreachable_platforms`, `unreachable_gifts`,
  `near_miss_gap`, `near_miss_rise`, `near_miss_excess` per level.
- `tools/pattern-audit/run.mjs` gained metric **`5.reachability`**:
  - **fails** if any gift sits on an unreachable platform (threshold 0)
  - reports unreachable platform count as informational — a decorative pillar
    with no gift on it is scenery, not a defect
  - reports the tightest rejected jump with its seed/level/players so it can
    be reproduced

## Tests

Four, in `levelgen::tests`:

- `reachability_report_flags_a_block_no_jump_can_reach` — a 4.5 u block is
  unreachable and reports **no** near-miss, because the rejection is on
  height, not gap.
- `reachability_report_clears_a_block_inside_the_envelope` — the same block at
  a 1.5 u rise is reachable and nothing is rejected.
- `reachability_report_measures_a_gap_too_wide_to_jump` — a 1.0 u block 6.5 u
  away is unreachable and reports `(6.5, -0.5, 1.5)`.
- `reachability_report_counts_gifts_on_unreachable_blocks` — a gift on the
  unreachable block is counted, and `validate` refuses that level.
