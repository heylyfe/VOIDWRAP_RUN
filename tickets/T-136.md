# T-136 — Bot falls: edge or seam?

**Cycle:** 15 · **Agent:** orchestrator · **Status:** PASS

T-130 proved the playthrough bots fall off the map and that the game is not killing
them unfairly. It could not say *how* they fell, and the two ways have different
fixes: an **edge fall** is a navigation problem (the bot walked off), a **seam fall**
is a geometry problem (a platform was right there and the collision layer missed it).
This ticket makes the death log answer that question, then answers it.

## The measurement

`deathlog::Death` gained `seed`, `ground_dist` and `ground_inset`.

The first attempt measured the distance from the **death position** to the nearest
platform footprint. That measurement is worthless, and the first run proved it: a
fall from a platform at `y=0` to the kill plane at `y=-16` takes about a second, and
a player moving 6 u/s drifts 6 u sideways in that time. Every death reported
`edge_dist` 0.4–2.1 u, which is drift, not geometry.

The fix is to measure where the player **last had ground contact**, not where they
died. `Player` gained `last_ground: Vec3`, refreshed every tick while
`state.grounded` is true. On death:

- `ground_dist` — horizontal distance from `last_ground` to the nearest platform
  footprint. ~0 means they were standing on something.
- `ground_inset` — how far *inside* that footprint `last_ground` was (distance to the
  nearest of the four sides). Negative means outside.

A player who walked off the edge leaves from `inset` ~0. A player who fell through a
seam leaves from well inside the platform, so **a large positive `inset` on a void
death is the seam signature**.

## The answer: no seam falls

90 s, 8 clients, seed `1196398494`, level 1 — 7 deaths, all void, all ending a run:

| player | y | vy | ground_dist | ground_inset |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 5 | -16.81 | -32.53 | 0.230 | -0.230 |
| 6 | -16.81 | -32.53 | 0.248 | -0.248 |
| 7 | -16.81 | -32.53 | 0.228 | -0.228 |
| 8 | -15.60 | -21.60 | 0.312 | -0.312 |
| 3 | -16.27 | -32.00 | 0.479 | -0.479 |
| 2 | -16.81 | -32.53 | 0.212 | -0.212 |
| 1 | -16.81 | -32.53 | 0.282 | -0.282 |

Every `inset` is **negative** and small: each player's last ground contact was
0.21–0.48 u *outside* a platform footprint. That is the coyote window — the
controller keeps `grounded` true for a few frames after the lip, so `last_ground`
lands just past the edge. It is the exact signature of a legitimate edge fall.

**Deaths with `inset > 0.5`: 0 of 7.** No player fell through geometry.

This agrees with the sim, which already tests the seam hypothesis directly and
passes: `generated_levels_are_completable_with_the_real_controller` and
`every_graph_edge_kind_is_physically_doable` walk the real controller across every
platform seam in generated levels and require it to arrive. A seam that dropped
players would fail those tests.

## What this means

The falls are the playthrough bots' random-walk navigation, not a collision bug.
Nothing to fix in the collision layer. The remaining question is the one T-130
already raised and this ticket sharpens: **the first fall of a run is always fatal**
(`Run::new` starts `lives: 0`, and the only spare is the rare `spare_wick` upgrade),
so a bot that wanders off once is out. That is a balance decision, not a bug, and it
stays a tuning note for the owner's balancing pass.

## Tuning notes (not changed)

- First fall of a run is fatal. Consider starting `lives: 1`, or making `spare_wick`
  less rare, so a single misstep does not end an 8-player run.
- The playthrough bots have no edge awareness at all; they walk in a random
  direction until they fall. That is why `level-progression` stays a known failure.
  A bot that steers toward the nearest gift would exercise far more of the game.
