# T-146 — The straggler beam wore LURE's colour

**Cycle:** 20 · **Agent:** orchestrator (deepseek-v4) · **Status:** PASS

## Why

T-112 added the straggler highlight: when at most 20% of a level's normal gifts remain,
every remaining gift gets a vertical beam so the endgame is not "wander an empty level
looking for one gift". JUDGMENT CALL 2 required that highlight to be *clearly different
from the normal glow, golden gifts, and LURE's glimmer*.

It was not. Measured in CIELAB:

| pair | delta-E |
|---|---|
| normal gift glow `#dfe6ff` vs **straggler beam** `#dfe6ff` | **0.0** |
| normal gift glow `#dfe6ff` vs **LURE shimmer** `#dfe6ff` | **0.0** |
| straggler beam `#dfe6ff` vs **LURE shimmer** `#dfe6ff` | **0.0** |

`BEAM_COLOR` was byte-identical to `COMMON.gift.glow`, and `Lure.tsx` wears
`COMMON.gift.glow` for its disguise. So the marker whose entire job is to say **"this one
is real"** was wearing the fake's colour.

The original comment reasoned its way into this:

> A beam, not a colour change: colour already carries normal-vs-golden, and LURE's
> shimmer wears the normal glow (JUDGMENT CALL 2), so a third colour would collide with
> both.

The premise is wrong. A third colour collides only if it is badly chosen. `#7cff4f` sits
**43.1** delta-E from the nearest colour in the entire game — the closest thing to it is
`blight`'s accent `#a3c34a`, and 43 is well past the point where two colours read as one.

## Change

1. **`web/src/scene/giftPalette.ts`** (new, pure, no three.js/React): the gift identity
   colours move out of `GiftBox.tsx` so they can be measured — `GOLD_BODY`,
   `GOLD_RIBBON`, `GOLD_BOW`, `GOLD_EMISSIVE`, `SPARKLE_COLOR`, `BEAM_COLOR`, and
   `BEAM_MIN_DELTA_E = 30`.
2. **`BEAM_COLOR` `#dfe6ff` → `#7cff4f`.** A saturated lime column, unmistakably an
   objective marker, and outside every other colour in the game.
3. **`GiftBox.tsx`** imports the constants instead of declaring them; the beam comment now
   records why the old reasoning was wrong.
4. **`web/src/scene/giftPalette.test.ts`** (new, 5 tests) asserts the beam is at least 30
   delta-E from the normal glow (and therefore LURE), the golden gift, the beacon, and
   every one of the 20 enemy accents — plus that the golden gift stays distinct from the
   normal gift.

## Negative test

Reverting `BEAM_COLOR` to `#dfe6ff` fails three of the five tests:

```
AssertionError: expected '#dfe6ff' not to be '#dfe6ff'
AssertionError: expected 15.64286971119836 to be greater than or equal to 30
AssertionError: expected [ Array(3) ] to deeply equal []
```

The third is the enemy-accent check naming three clashes. Restored, 5/5 pass.

## Choice log

- **Changed the beam, not the normal glow.** The pale blue-white glow is the normal gift's
  identity and LURE's disguise depends on it; the beam is the new thing and the one that
  must stand apart.
- **Did not change LURE.** JUDGMENT CALL 2 says LURE mimics a *normal* gift, and it does —
  correctly. The defect was that the highlight joined it.
- **Moved the constants rather than reading `GiftBox.tsx` from the test.** The enemy
  palette guard reads model sources because those are 20 hand-written JSX files; here
  there is one file, so a pure module is the smaller and cleaner fix.
- **`BEAM_MIN_DELTA_E = 30`, not 20.** The beam is a large, bright, additive-blended
  column rather than a small emissive trim, so it needs more separation than an accent
  does to avoid reading as a tint of its neighbour.

## Tuning notes

The beam is now a saturated lime rather than a pale blue. If it proves too loud in play,
the lever is `BEAM_COLOR`'s lightness (a darker `#4fbf2f` keeps the hue separation) — not
the floor, which is what makes the marker trustworthy.
